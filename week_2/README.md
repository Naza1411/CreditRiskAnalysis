## Comparing Flat-Rate and Reducing-Balance Loan Repayment Methods

- Cedar Microfinance Bank can use both flat-rate and reducing-balance methods to calculate loan repayments, but each method affects customers differently. Under the flat-rate method, interest is calculated on the original loan principal for the entire loan term, regardless of how much of the principal has already been repaid. This results in equal interest charges throughout the repayment period and generally leads to a higher total repayment amount.

- The reducing-balance method calculates interest only on the outstanding principal after each repayment. As the loan balance decreases, the interest charged also declines, meaning a larger portion of each instalment goes toward repaying the principal over time. Although the quoted annual interest rate may be the same as a flat-rate loan, the total interest paid is usually lower, making reducing-balance loans more affordable for borrowers.

- The quoted interest rate alone does not always represent the true borrowing cost because customers may also pay processing fees, documentation charges, insurance premiums, and other mandatory costs. In addition, the timing of repayments affects the actual cost of borrowing. For this reason, Cedar MFB should disclose the effective borrowing cost, total repayment amount, and repayment schedule alongside the quoted rate. Providing this information improves transparency, enables customers to compare loan products accurately, supports informed borrowing decisions, and strengthens customer confidence while promoting responsible and fair lending practices.
